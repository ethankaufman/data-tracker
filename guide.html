What this site is

Every day this site records two things about the Premier League season: what a prediction market thinks will happen (who wins the title, who goes down, who finishes in the top 4, 6 and 10), and the league table. It keeps every past day, so you can see how opinion moves as the season goes on.

It is a fan and class project. It is not betting advice and is not connected to Polymarket, ESPN or the Premier League.

How to read a price

In a prediction market, people buy and sell "Yes" shares for a question such as "Will Arsenal win the title?". A Yes share pays $1 if it happens and nothing if it does not. So the price people agree on shows how likely they think it is.

Example: a Yes share trading at $0.25 means the traders, putting real money behind their opinions, think there is about a 25% chance. This site shows prices as percentages. Price, chance and probability all mean the same thing here.

A price is an opinion, not a promise. A club at 5% still wins the title one time in twenty, if the market is right.

Changes

NumberWhat it meansHow to read it
pp (percentage points)The unit for a change in a percentage.A price going from 30% to 35% is +5 pp (not "+5%", and not +16.7%).
ChangeHow much a price moved since the previous day with data, in pp. ▲ is up and ▼ is down.The arrow and the + or − sign say the direction; the colour is just extra.

Page by page

Today

  • Last run: when the daily update last ran (in UTC and in your own time), and which day the data is from.
  • Counts and sources: explained above.
  • Biggest moves: the 10 biggest price changes since the previous run. On the first day there is nothing to compare with.
  • Latest values: every price with its change since the previous run. Use the tabs to switch market.

Browse

  • Search matches club names. Filters narrow by market, price range, spread, and whether the item traded in the last 24 hours.
  • Sort by clicking any column heading. Click again to flip between high-to-low and low-to-high.
  • Stars save an item to your shortlist. It is stored in this browser on this device (there are no accounts), so it is still there when you come back. Tick "Shortlist only" to see just your starred items.

Table

  • The full league table, sortable by every column, with a search box. Click a club for its detailed panel. The address changes to include the club, so you can share a link straight to it.

Trends

  • Every value over the days: pick a value (price, spread, volume...) and up to 8 clubs. Hover over the chart, or use the left and right arrow keys, to read each day.
  • Findings: five short reports, explained next. Every number is worked out in your browser from the history file.
  • Show the numbers: under every chart, a table with the exact figures.

Install and offline

The site can be installed like an app (look for "Install app" at the top, or use your browser's install option). Offline, it still opens and shows the last data it loaded.

The five findings on Trends

  1. The title favourite. Which club the title market rates highest, and how much of the 100% of title chances the top three hold between them.
  2. How fair the markets are. For each market, the sum of its prices against the fair total (100% for the title, 300% for relegation, and so on). The gap is the overround. A bigger gap usually means a thinner, less certain market.
  3. Biggest change since the first run. The item whose price has moved most since the first day, in pp, with how many items rose and fell. It fills in from day two.
  4. The biggest one-day move. Between each pair of days, the single biggest price change, and the biggest of all. It fills in from day two.
  5. The hardest market to trade. The average spread in each market. The wider the spread, the less the prices can be trusted to the decimal.

Each finding says exactly how it is computed from the history file.

Where the data comes from

  • Market prices come from Polymarket's public data, one download per market.
  • The league table comes from ESPN's public feed.
  • Once a day, an automated job (on GitHub) downloads all six, saves the raw files, rebuilds the history, writes a daily report on GitHub, and updates this site. The files the site reads are data/history.csv and data/status.json. The code and a full description of every column are in the project's repository.

Good to know

Why do a market's prices add up to more than they should?

Traders and sellers keep a small cushion, and thinly traded markets are rough. That extra is the overround (see above). It is normal and it is why finding 2 exists.

Why do some clubs show 0.05%?

That is about the lowest price the market lists. It means "the market sees almost no chance", not exactly zero.

Why is a spread so wide in some markets?

Less popular markets (often the Top 6 and Top 10 ones) can have very few orders waiting. With a large gap between buyers and sellers, the listed price is only a rough guide.

Why is a value a dash (–)?

The source did not report it that day, for example no trades in the last 24 hours.

Why didn't the league table change today?

The table only changes after matches, and there are quiet days and international breaks. Market prices can move on any day, which is why the site tracks both.

Is this betting advice?

No. It shows what a market thinks, which can be wrong.
